I built a few websites with Muse.

I have been using Muse and here are some things that I have learned and accomplished so far.

I’m not a programmer. I’m a regular guy who got curious about what AI could actually do, so I started building real websites with Muse, the AI I work with, and I’ve been learning as I go ever since.

Every site you’ll find linked from here started the same way: an idea I had, a conversation with Muse, and a lot of “let’s try it and see.”

Why I’m doing this

First and foremost, this is educational for me. I wanted to understand this technology by using it, not just reading about it. Building a website is a great teacher. You find out pretty quickly what works, what doesn’t, and what to ask for next time.

But I’d be lying if I said it was only about learning. I think of it as helping to forge the future in a small way, putting real things out into the world, for my family, my church, and anyone else who finds them useful.

I have already been using different AI tools at work. They really help me organize and streamline certain mundane tasks. That made me want to see what I could do and how I could use AI on a personal level, besides editing photos.

I started out just wanting to learn. I kept going because I could see what was possible.

How Muse helps me

Muse is the one who does the building for me. I don’t write the code. I describe what I want, in plain words, and Muse builds it, explains what it’s doing, and changes it when I ask. If I don’t like how something looks or works, I say so, and we fix it together.

Muse does all the work. I just give a few prompts.

  1. 1

    I bring the idea

    “I want a site about the Moon.” “I want daily devotionals my family can read.” “I want to hear my favorite worship songs.” It always starts with something I actually care about.

  2. 2

    Muse builds it

    Muse writes the code, designs the pages, finds the photos and information, and gets the site working, then shows it to me so I can check it with my own eyes before it goes anywhere.

  3. 3

    We explore what else it can do

    That’s my favorite part. Muse helps me try out different functionality I wouldn’t have known to ask about, and half the time, one experiment turns into the next site.

Things we’ve tried along the way

Each site taught me something new, because each one needed Muse to do something different:

  • •A Moon site that knows what tonight looks like: it works out the Moon’s phase from the date, every time you open it.
  • •Devotionals that show the right day on their own: a new reading each morning for men, women, families, and teenagers.
  • •A worship music library: search for songs, hear a preview, and build my own playlists. My Spotify liked songs even made the trip over.
  • •A sports page for my teams: the Lions, Tigers, Pistons, Red Sox, and UConn, all in one place.
  • •A game, just because we could: Moonlight Run, with levels, enemies, and a character wearing my Westchester hat.

What I’ve built so far

Luna, Earth’s Moon

Where it really took off. The Moon is one of my favorite things, so we built it a proper home with tonight’s phase, photos, facts, and even a corner for The Expanse.

Daily Devotionals

This one started off as a daily devotional for men, just for myself. From there it branched out into the other areas of life I felt were important, so now there are four sets: for men, for women, for families, and for teenagers. A fresh reading every day, each with the passage in KJV, ESV, and NLT.

Worship Music

My own place to find worship songs, listen to previews, and keep playlists, including the songs I’ve liked on Spotify over the years.

Sports Teams

One page for all my teams, so I’m not hunting around five different places to keep up with them.

Moonlight Run

A game we built just to see if we could, full of running, jumping, climbing, and dodging enemies themed around paperwork across four levels, with maybe more levels still to come. It might be my favorite experiment of the bunch.

This page

Even this About page was built the same way. I told Muse what I wanted it to say, and here it is.

Where these sites live

Once Muse builds a site for me, it needs a home, and that’s GitHub. Think of GitHub as a free place on the internet to store a website’s files. It keeps everything safe and organized, and through GitHub Pages it actually puts the sites online, so anyone with the link can open them.

It doesn’t cost me anything, which still amazes me a little. I build with Muse, the sites live on GitHub for free, and they’re just… there, ready whenever I want to show somebody.

What’s next

Honestly? I don’t know yet, and that’s kind of the point. I’m still learning, still asking Muse “can we do this?” and still being surprised by the answer. There’s more to come. When I think of the next thing, we’ll build it, and it’ll end up linked below with the rest.

Thanks for stopping by and taking a look at what an ordinary person and an AI can put together.

Eric Younger
